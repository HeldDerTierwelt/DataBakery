# What your sales data can tell us about tomorrow's baking

**For:** the operations manager of the Kiel branch · **From:** team DataBakery (Christine Avery, Zhouling Zhang, Alexander Goy) · **As of:** 22 Nov 2026

**In short:** five years of daily sales are enough to forecast tomorrow's revenue per product group, including weekends, holidays and Kiel Week. They cannot show when something sold out, and they give revenue, not pieces. For pieces we need your average prices.

## What we work with

| Data | What it contains | Period |
|---|---|---|
| Your sales | Revenue per day for each of 6 product groups: bread, rolls, croissants, confectionery, cakes, seasonal bread | Jul 2013 – Jul 2018, about 1,800 opening days |
| Weather in Kiel | Temperature, cloud cover, wind, rain or snow per day | same period |
| Kiel Week | The 9 Kiel Week days of each year | 5 Kiel Weeks (2014–2018) |
| Public holidays | Schleswig-Holstein public holidays, from a standard calendar package | same period |

The forecast is meant for your daily plan at about 14:00 for the next day. It only uses what you know at that moment: sales up to the day before yesterday, the calendar, and the weather forecast.

## What the data already shows

- **Weekends sell about a quarter more than weekdays in total**, but not for every product: bread is weakest on Sunday, while rolls, croissants and cakes peak on Saturday and Sunday.
- **Open public holidays behave like a strong Sunday.** On Easter Monday, Ascension Day, Whit Monday and German Unity Day you sold about twice the usual rolls, croissants and confectionery, a third more cakes, and about 40 % less bread than on a normal day with the same weekday. "Same day last week" misses badly on these days.
- **New Year's Eve is the most unusual day of the year**: cakes sell about 7 times their usual amount.
- **Revenue fell by about a sixth from 2014 to 2016** and has stayed flat since. The forecast takes this into account.

All of these are patterns in past sales. They show what usually happens, not why.

## What the data cannot tell

- **When something sold out.** Sales stop when the shelf is empty, so on such days true demand was higher than the revenue shows. We found no sign of frequent sell-outs, but we cannot rule them out.
- **Leftovers and waste.** They are not in the data.
- **Pieces.** The data contains revenue only. We assume it is in €; the source does not say.
- **Effects of prices, new products, roadworks, local events** other than Kiel Week, or other branches.
- **Rare days are thin.** Christmas, Easter, Kiel Week and the seasonal-bread season occur only 5 times in the data, so forecasts for them are less certain.
- **Closed days** (e.g. Christmas, New Year, Good Friday) are not forecast.

## How we will judge the forecast

We compare it with "bake what sold on the same weekday last week", which we assume is roughly how the plan is made today. The forecast is only worth using if it is clearly closer to actual sales on a full year it has never seen (Aug 2017 – Jul 2018): at least 10 % smaller errors overall, and better for bread, rolls and cakes.

## What we need from you

1. **Average price per product group**, to turn revenue into pieces.
2. **How you plan today.** Is it really "same day last week"?
3. **Is running out worse than leftovers**, and for which products? This decides whether to bake above the forecast.
4. **Two puzzles in the data:** on 53 days in 2013–2016 there is no confectionery entry although the shop was open. Was it not offered, or not recorded? And why did confectionery sell unusually well on some Sundays in February?
